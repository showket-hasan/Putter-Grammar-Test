Section 3: Stylistic Analysis & Rhetorical Effect (The "A-Level" Core)

[10 Marks • Q21–Q30]

Examiner Focus: Crucial for A-Level English Language and Literature. Assesses the analytical capacity to evaluate WHY authors leverage specific grammatical choices to manipulate tone, agency, ideology, and perspective.

21. "The protesters were arrested following the demonstration."

In journalistic writing, what is the primary rhetorical effect of using the passive voice here instead of the active voice ("Police arrested the protesters")?

22. "The implementation of the new policy caused widespread confusion." (vs. "They implemented the policy and confused people.")

What is the effect of nominalization (turning verbs into nouns) in academic and political discourse?

23. "Never before had I witnessed such profound institutional decay."

Why would a novelist or speechwriter use negative adverbial inversion in this sentence?

24. "We must navigate this economic storm together."

How does the combination of the modal verb of obligation and the metaphor function rhetorically?

25. "What infuriated the public was the CEO's multi-million-dollar bonus."

What is the communicative purpose of using a Wh- pseudo-cleft construction here?

26. "The factory closed last Tuesday." (vs. "The corporation closed the factory.")

What is the ideological effect of using an ergative verb (where the object of an action becomes the subject of an intransitive verb) in corporate reporting?

27. "It is undeniably clear that the data somewhat suggests a correlation."

What is the strategic effect of combining a booster ("undeniably clear") with a hedge ("somewhat suggests")?

28. "He stared at the ticking clock. Would she ever arrive? The train must have left by now."

In literature, what is the effect of Free Indirect Discourse (blending third-person narration with first-person internal thought)?

29. "The more the politician deflected, the less the public trusted him."

What does this correlative comparative structure achieve?

30. "Dropping his briefcase, sprinting down the platform, he barely caught the departing train."

How do these fronted non-finite participle clauses affect the narrative pacing?
